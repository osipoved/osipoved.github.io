Informational Content of Auction Prices

with Yu Awaya and Vijay Krishna · Job market paper

[paper] [HTML]

We compare how much information discriminatory and uniform-price auctions convey to outside observers. Using the Lehmann criterion, we identify conditions under which the highest price in a discriminatory auction is more informative than the uniform price. The results connect auction design to the informativeness of order statistics.

Voting with Population Uncertainty: A Poissonized Order-Statistics Approach

I study collective decisions when participation is uncertain and follows a Poisson distribution. A complete Lehmann ranking of Poissonized order statistics characterizes when unanimity yields higher symmetric equilibrium welfare. I also examine how state-dependent participation affects information aggregation and when unanimity makes aggregation robust to this uncertainty.

Private Learning and Strategic Delay

I study a coordination game in which players privately learn through conclusive good news. The incentive to wait is nonmonotonic in the rate of learning: informed players act promptly when learning is rare or sufficiently frequent, while intermediate learning rates create strategic delay in every equilibrium.
